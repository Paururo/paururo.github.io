01 · research

What I work on

two pathogens, one thread

Tuberculosis and COVID-19 look like different problems, but their genomic data share a trap: much of what we can conclude is decided before any hypothesis is tested, by the reference genome we map to in tuberculosis and by which samples get sequenced in SARS-CoV-2. My work tries to measure those biases and build methods that hold up despite them.

Tuberculosis: lineages, resistance and hosts

The M. tuberculosis complex is split into lineages that differ in where they circulate and which hosts they infect. I build tools that type lineages and resistance the same way across surveillance programmes, and I study how the match between strain and host changes the way macrophages respond to infection.

Pathotypr, 2026 Host compatibility, 2026 Lineage 9, 2021

SARS-CoV-2: evolution and surveillance

From the first pandemic wave in Spain to Omicron, I have followed spike mutations that arise again and again and tested whether they matter. Lately I compare wastewater with clinical sequencing: hospital wastewater caught KP.3 about three months before routine clinical surveillance did.

mBio, 2021 PLOS Pathogens, 2022 Wastewater, 2026

Open-source tools for pathogen genomics

I write fast, low-memory command-line tools, mostly in Rust, and reproducible Nextflow and Snakemake pipelines. Six of them are on Bioconda, downloaded about 7,000 times so far, and one R package is on CRAN.

Science, drawn

I also illustrate: graphical abstracts, outreach pieces for the institute and the logo at the top of this page. A clear figure is often what makes a genomics result travel beyond the people who already work on it.
